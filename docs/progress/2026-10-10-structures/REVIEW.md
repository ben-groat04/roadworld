# Cold-read review: structures and earthworks, first renders (10 Oct 2026)

Reviewer: cold read of the 16 PNGs. Compared against `docs/reference/aerial/26_a9_gelly_overbridge_earthworks_scotland.jpg` (overbridge with piers, sloped approaches, wing walls), `28_a3_hindhead_tunnel_portals_cutting_uk.jpg` (portals set into a graded cutting, low retaining walls beside the carriageway), and by name the street set 04/05/06 (walls, gabions, cuttings) and 10-14 (cuttings, embankments). Materials and lighting are not judged.

## Verdicts on the agent's flags
- **Wall tops are a wide slab, not thin coping: CONFIRMED** (s4_aerial, s7_aerial: pale band the full wall depth along the top edge; s4_street, s7_street: a thick light band under the brick). Real coping is 0.3-0.5 m and reads as a line. Geometry/rule fault (structure_mesh).
- **Vertical striping on the steep slope in s4: CONFIRMED** (s4_aerial right/lower third, s7_aerial lower right: dense vertical hatching, plus ragged stair-stepped edge near the wall end). Cause: slope faces sampled on a regular texel grid and rendered at coarse export step. Export tool/viewer artefact, though the stepped edge at the wall end is also a grading-resolution fault.
- **Sawtooth geometry at s8 slip roads: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** At the slip merges (s8_aerial, lower left near the dual carriageway start, and top right) the road edge is ragged and earthworks end in abrupt wedges. Junctions plus export-tool step (2 m) both contribute.
- **Walls very tall: CONFIRMED.** s4_street wall is the full left field of view, easily 8-10 m against a 3 m wide-ish lane; s7 shows two stacked tiers of the same height. Real roadside retaining walls are low with a slope above (street/05 gabions about 1.5-2 m; street/04 concrete block end about 3-4 m, with a separate 6-8 m netted rock cut beyond it; checked against the images 11 Oct 2026). SCENARIO choice (0.85 and 0.45 slope sidehills force it) but also a RULE fault: the rule "walls only where a slope cannot fit" is evidently allowing 10 m walls instead of benching or a long 1:2 cut slope.
- **s2 block hard to read: CONFIRMED**, see below.

## Per scenario

**s1 bridge over road, 6/10.** Right: two stout piers, abutment wing walls, approach embankments rising to the deck, parapet line visible (street, centre). Wrong: (1) the piers stand in the clear opening on a road with no central reservation pocket; they sit where the lower road passes, with a visible gap of only roughly one lane each side (street, centre): a pier blocking the carriageway axis is plausible only for a dual road, here it is a two-way rural road, so clearance looks tight. Rule/scenario. (2) Embankments are very tall and short, with rounded crest but a hard crease at the toe (street right, big mound). Compare 26: approaches are broad, gentle, and the abutment sits back. Grading. (3) Deck reads as a thin plank, no visible beam depth; parapet and deck merge in one concrete lump (street). structure_mesh. (4) Aerial: parapet shows as a separate floating strip beside the deck on the right of the bridge. Looks detached from the deck edge. structure_mesh. 

**s2 short-gap solid block, 4/10.** Right: ditch is crossed, no piers per rule. Wrong: the "block" is not read as a block: street shows two flat concrete slabs standing above road level like freestanding walls with a gap between (centre and right), and the left one has a tapering triangular underside. Looks like two parapets, with the earth mound under it. A solid short-span structure should be a headwall-bounded culvert/fill: fill carried right up to road level with headwalls at the ditch. Aerial: the right parapet is detached from the road, floating beyond the bank with a gap, the left parapet touches the carriageway edge. Asymmetric and mismatched. Also the ditch cut ends abruptly at a rounded lip left of the bridge (aerial). structure_mesh (block fill missing under road, parapet offset) and grading (ditch crossing).

**s3 tunnel portal, 6/10.** Best-composed scene. Right: portal headwall set into the hill, wing walls with stepped tops, a bore visible, road ramps into it, approach cut blends into hill (street). Wrong: (1) the tunnel mouth is rectangular-with-shallow-arch and very wide against the headwall; real portals (28) are arched with the arch taking the whole face and a thin lip. (2) The hill is a smooth analytic dome with no cutting: the road enters the dome in a shallow notch, while 28 shows a deep V-cut approach with slope on both sides; here the notch is only a few metres deep (aerial, shadow only). Grading/scenario. (3) Wing walls are tan brick-textured stepped blocks; their stepped top looks cartoonish (street, left and right of portal) and they do not splay. structure_mesh. (4) Aerial: the road past the hill (top right) simply stops at the other side of the dome with a raised embankment, with no matching exit portal; the road looks to hop over the hill. Scenario.

**s4 retaining wall run, 3/10.** Right: wall follows the road, continuous. Wrong: wall height ~10 m with a thick slab top; zero visible 4 m panel steps (street: perfectly smooth diagonal top, no steps; the brief said panels step at 1.5% gradient). Rule not visible; either steps are below render resolution or not implemented. Wall start (aerial, right end) terminates with a stubby jagged stair and the slope beyond is a clean-cut vertical face with striping. Real walls (street/04) have visible joints, weep holes and a end pier. No verge strip between wall and carriageway at all: a 1.5 m verge rule appears violated (street: wall rises straight off the lane edge, only a grass sliver at foot). Hmm, there is a thin green strip; narrow and uneven. structure_mesh/grading; striping is export tool.

**s5 cutting, 6/10.** Right: smooth rounded crests, cutting symmetric, plausible gentle batter, road legible (street). Wrong: slopes are too shallow and low to read as a cutting at all; ridge is 10 m but the crest sits barely 6-8 m above the road (street, both sides), and the slope is a uniform plane with no berm or ditch at the toe (street/10 shows a 3 m grass and heather toe strip; street/12 shows a rough verge with no ditch visible: unverified against the image). No verge strip visible beside the carriageway. Aerial: shows almost nothing, a faint lighter smear; useless view. grading (no toe ditch), viewer (camera).

**s6 embankment, 2/10 as a render.** Street image shows nothing but a flat field: the camera is evidently below or beside the embankment and no road is in frame; the one purpose (rounded toe) is not visible. Aerial: reads as a long dark slope under a thin road with a sharp end where the embankment simply stops at the right (top right: abrupt lip, dark wedge, no ramp end taper). The embankment end is a vertical-looking cut rather than a tapering toe into ground. Compare street/13 and 14: gentle 1:2-1:3 grass bank with fence. Scenario (camera placement) first; the abrupt end is grading.

**s7 adjacent levels, 3/10.** Right: two roads at different levels are separated by structure rather than one giant wall. Wrong: it is two ~8-10 m brick walls stacked with a narrow bench (street, left), which is exactly the giant-wall outcome the scenario was meant to avoid, just split in two. Same slab coping, same lack of panel steps. Aerial: both walls run to a common end where the lower road's end has a ragged vertical cut face with striping and the upper road ends against a dark trench. Real practice: terraced slopes with 1:2 batters, one wall at most 3 m. Rule: walls chosen over slope when the slope would fit; scenario is steep, but the grader should step the slope instead.

**s8 interchange, 5/10.** Right: reads as a real roundabout interchange: dual carriageway, two slip roads each side, overbridge, roundabout, kerbed splitter islands with continuity (street: kerbed slip diverge is the nicest road image in the set, and compare 05/11 aerials). Wrong: (1) the minor road bridge is a tiny pair of slabs at the horizon (street) and no earthwork rises to it: approaches look flat, the road climbs to nothing with grass at the same level to either side (street, left and right: the slip roads below on the sides appear at lower level than the bridge approach but with a hard vertical grass step). (2) Aerial: no embankments anywhere, so the overbridge approaches are not on fill; dark patches only near the bridge. (3) Slip road ends meet the mainline as abrupt tapered wedges with no merge taper or kerb (aerial lower left and top right), jagged edges. junctions/export tool. (4) Roundabout is tiny relative to the approaches and the circulatory carriageway looks one lane; fine as a test.

## Overall: 4.5/10
Layout and road geometry are credible; the earthworks and walls are the weak part: wall height and coping, no panel steps, missing toe ditches, abrupt ends.

## Top 8 fixes
1. Walls: stop choosing 8-10 m walls; prefer slope/benching, cap wall height (~3 m) and add retaining only beyond that. (grading)
2. Coping to 0.3-0.5 m thin line and add visible panel joints and 0.5 m steps. (structure_mesh)
3. Grading ends: taper embankment/cutting ends and wall ends into terrain; no vertical faces. (grading)
4. s2 short-span: fill block under road with headwalls; fix asymmetric floating parapet. (structure_mesh)
5. Add approach embankments and abutment setback for the interchange bridge and s1; gentler 1:2-1:3 slopes. (grading/junctions)
6. Slip road to mainline merge tapers and edges. (junctions)
7. Finer terrain step (<=0.5 m) on slope faces; fix striping. (export tool)
8. Re-aim s5/s6 cameras and add cutting toe ditch plus 1.5 m verge between wall and lane. (test scenario / grading)
